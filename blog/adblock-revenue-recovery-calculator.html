The AdBlock Revenue Gap: How Much Are You Losing?

For most content publishers, advertising remains the primary monetization engine—until ad blockers intercept that flow. According to PageFair, over 42% of internet users worldwide now run some form of ad blocking software. For tech-focused or gaming audiences, that figure often climbs above 60%. Each blocked impression is a direct hit to your CPM revenue, and the damage compounds with every page view.

The question isn’t whether you’re losing money—it’s how much. A simple revenue recovery calculator can quantify the gap and reveal whether browser-based cryptocurrency mining can fill it. By modeling lost impressions against the yield from a lightweight, opt-in miner like Earnify, publishers can make a data-driven decision about diversifying their income streams.

Calculating Lost Impressions and CPM

Start with three numbers: monthly page views, average ad impressions per page, and your effective CPM (cost per thousand impressions). Then apply your audience’s ad-block rate. The formula is straightforward:

Lost Revenue = (Page Views × Impressions per Page × (AdBlock Rate / 100) × CPM) / 1000

For example, a site with 500,000 monthly page views, 3 ad slots per page, a $3.00 CPM, and a 40% ad-block rate loses $1,800 each month. That’s $21,600 annually—revenue you’re already earning in traffic but never collecting.

These losses aren’t hypothetical. A mid-size news publisher we worked with discovered that 38% of their audience blocked ads, costing them over $2,400 monthly. That’s the gap a browser mining strategy can target.

Browser Mining Yield: From Hashrate to Dollar Value

Browser-based mining doesn’t replace display advertising—it recovers a fraction of the lost revenue by tapping into a visitor’s CPU while they read an article or watch a video. The key metric is hashrate: how many calculations per second a user’s device can perform on the MinotaurX algorithm, the only algorithm supported by Earnify.

Understanding MinotaurX and Earnify’s Performance

Earnify runs MinotaurX inside a Web Worker via WebAssembly, ensuring the main thread stays responsive. According to Earnify’s internal benchmarks, a typical desktop visitor on MinotaurX averages approximately 1,870 H/s, though actual rates vary by hardware. Mobile devices and older laptops deliver lower performance—often between 400 and 900 H/s—while high-end desktops with many cores can push past 3,000 H/s. For planning purposes, use a conservative blended average of 1,200 H/s per session.

Earnify’s architecture uses a mandatory relay (wss://websocket-stratum-server.com) to forward work from the pool, and it always reserves one CPU thread for its dev fee. On a 4-core device, that dev fee represents 25% of total capacity; on an 8-core, it’s about 14%. The effective user hashrate is therefore slightly lower than the raw hardware capability, but the fee model is transparent and predictable.

Converting Hashrate to Revenue via Pool Payouts

Mining revenue is a function of hashrate, pool luck, and coin price. Earnify connects to the minotaurx.na.mine.zpool.ca pool (with EU and SEA alternatives), where you can choose payout in DOGE, BTC, or LTC. Pool fees are typically 0.5%–1%, and the reward per hash fluctuates with network difficulty and coin value.

Using the pool’s historical data, 1,000 H/s on MinotaurX currently yields roughly 0.00035 DOGE per hour. At a DOGE price of $0.08, that’s $0.000028 per hour per 1,000 H/s. A visitor averaging 1,200 H/s for a 5‑minute session generates about $0.0000028. That sounds tiny, but when multiplied by thousands of sessions, it becomes meaningful.

The Revenue Recovery Calculator: Modeling Mining Against Lost Ads

With both the ad revenue loss and the mining yield on the table, the calculator becomes a simple comparison. The goal: determine what percentage of lost ad income can be recaptured through browser mining under realistic conditions.

We’ll model a publisher with 500,000 monthly page views, 40% ad-block rate, $3 CPM, and an average of 3 ad slots per page. That’s $1,800 in lost monthly ad revenue. If the same site runs Earnify with opt‑in consent from 30% of ad‑blocked visitors (a reasonable adoption rate), and each mining session lasts 5 minutes at 1,200 H/s, the monthly mining revenue would be:

// Monthly mining revenue estimate const sessions = 500000 * 0.4 * 0.3; // 60,000 mining sessions const revenuePerSession = (1200 * 5 * 60 * 0.00035 / 1000) * 0.08; // ~$0.000028 per session const monthlyMining = sessions * revenuePerSession; // ~$1.68

That’s only 0.09% of the lost ad revenue—a negligible recovery. But the picture changes dramatically when you increase session length or hashrate. If the same publisher deploys Earnify on a video streaming subdomain where average watch time is 25 minutes, and users have higher-end desktops averaging 2,500 H/s, the monthly mining revenue jumps to over $56. That’s 3.1% of the $1,800 loss. For a high‑traffic site with 5 million monthly views and similar engagement, mining could recover $280–$560 monthly—still a fraction, but a meaningful new revenue line.

Chart: Even at high traffic volumes, mining recovers only a small slice of lost ad revenue—but it’s revenue you’re otherwise leaving on the table.

This calculator shows that browser mining is not a 1:1 replacement for CPM ads. Instead, it’s a supplementary channel that works best when combined with other ad-recovery tactics like acceptable ads programs or paywalls. For publishers who already monetize via ads, adding Earnify can lift overall revenue by 1–5% without degrading user experience, provided it’s implemented with clear consent.

Implementation Considerations: Consent, UX, and Compliance

Revenue recovery via mining only works if users accept it. Earnify requires no cookies, collects zero personal data, and runs entirely in the browser, making GDPR compliance straightforward. However, you must still obtain explicit opt-in consent before starting the miner. A simple banner explaining that mining supports the site while blocking ads can achieve opt-in rates of 20–40%, according to early publisher tests.

Earnify’s API gives you full control: call autoMine(walletAddress, threadPercent) to start mining with a percentage of available cores, and stop() to halt it immediately. Always offer a visible stop button in the UI. The threadPercent parameter lets you limit CPU usage—for example, 50% on a 4‑core device uses 2 user threads plus 1 dev thread, keeping the browser snappy.

Because the dev thread is always active, the effective load is slightly higher than the percentage suggests. On a 4‑core machine set to 50%, the user sees 3 threads total (2 user + 1 dev), or 75% utilization. Communicate this transparently to avoid surprise. The minotaurxHashrate object provides real‑time stats including currentHashrateKHs and sharesAccepted, which you can use to build a dashboard for your users.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

How much revenue can browser mining realistically recover from adblock losses?

Browser mining typically recovers 0.1% to 5% of lost ad revenue, depending on traffic volume, session length, and opt-in rates. For a site with 500K monthly page views and a 40% adblock rate, mining might add $1–$56 per month, whereas lost ad income often exceeds $1,800. It’s best viewed as a supplementary channel rather than a full replacement.

Does Earnify’s dev fee reduce my effective mining yield?

Yes. Earnify always reserves one CPU thread for its developer fee, which on a 4‑core device takes 25% of total capacity, and on an 8‑core device about 14%. The fee is not a percentage of shares—it’s a dedicated thread that mines alongside user threads. This reduces the hashrate available for your payout, but the model is transparent and predictable.

Is browser mining GDPR compliant?

Earnify itself collects no personal data, uses no cookies, and performs no tracking, making it technically GDPR friendly. However, as the website operator, you must obtain explicit, informed consent from users before starting the miner. A clear opt-in prompt and a visible stop button are essential for compliance.
