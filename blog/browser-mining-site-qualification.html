Is Your Site a Good Candidate for Browser Mining? A Qualification Checklist

Not every website benefits from browser-based crypto mining. Use this 10-point checklist to evaluate if your traffic, content, and audience make MinotaurX mining profitable.

Browser mining sounds like a dream: visitors contribute spare CPU cycles, you earn crypto, and nobody sees an ad. But the reality is more nuanced. According to Earnify's 2026 publisher data, sites that qualify on at least 7 of 10 criteria in this checklist see 3.2× higher average revenue per session than those that skip the evaluation and deploy blindly.

This isn't about whether browser mining works — it does. It's about whether your specific site has the right traffic profile, content structure, and audience behavior to make MinotaurX mining a meaningful revenue channel rather than a rounding error. Let's walk through the qualification framework.

1. Traffic Volume: Do You Have Enough Sessions?

Browser mining is a volume game. Each visitor contributes a tiny amount of hashrate for the duration of their session. Without enough aggregate session-minutes, earnings won't justify the implementation effort.

Earnify Benchmark: Publishers with fewer than 5,000 monthly pageviews typically generate less than $2/month from browser mining. At 50,000+ monthly pageviews, the same implementation averages $18–$45/month depending on session duration and device mix.

Checklist item #1: Does your site receive at least 10,000 monthly pageviews? If yes, give yourself a point. Below 5,000, browser mining alone won't move the needle — consider it a supplementary layer alongside display ads or affiliate content.

2. Session Duration: How Long Do Visitors Stay?

Mining revenue is directly proportional to time-on-page. A visitor who bounces in 3 seconds contributes essentially zero hashes. A visitor who reads a 2,000-word article for 4 minutes contributes meaningful work.

Earnify's minotaurxHashrate API tracks cumulative hashes per session. Internal data from 1,200+ publisher domains shows a near-linear relationship between session duration and shares submitted to the pool.

Average MinotaurX shares submitted per session by time-on-page bracket. Source: Earnify publisher telemetry, Q1 2026.

Checklist item #2: Is your average session duration above 45 seconds? Check Google Analytics: Behavior → Site Content → All Pages, then sort by Avg. Time on Page. If your top 10 landing pages all exceed 45 seconds, give yourself a point.

Sites that excel here: long-form blogs, documentation portals, educational platforms, web-based tools, and forums. Sites that struggle: news aggregators, meme galleries, and link directories where users click away instantly.

3. Device Profile: Desktop vs. Mobile Split

This is the single most decisive factor. Browser mining runs on Web Workers compiled to WebAssembly. Desktop CPUs — even older ones — deliver 8–15× the hashrate of mobile SoCs due to higher core counts, better thermals, and no OS-level throttling of background threads.

Checklist item #3: Is your desktop traffic share above 35%? Check GA: Audience → Technology → Browser & OS, segment by device category. Above 35% desktop earns a point. Above 60% desktop, give yourself two points — you're in the sweet spot.

3.1 Geographic CPU Power Correlation

Device profile correlates with geography. Visitors from North America, Western Europe, and East Asia tend to have newer hardware with higher core counts. According to Earnify's 2026 hashrate benchmarks, a desktop visitor on MinotaurX averages 1,870 H/s in the US and Germany, but only 1,240 H/s in Brazil and 980 H/s in India — reflecting older average hardware.

If your analytics show 70%+ traffic from Tier-1 hardware markets, your effective revenue-per-session jumps 40–60% compared to a global-average audience. This isn't about excluding anyone; it's about understanding where your revenue concentration actually sits.

4. Content Type: Does Your Site Encourage Lingering?

Not all content types are equal candidates. The ideal content for browser mining keeps users on a single page for extended periods without demanding GPU resources or causing fan noise complaints.

Strong candidates:

  • Long-form articles and tutorials (1,500+ words)
  • Documentation and knowledge bases
  • Web-based tools and calculators (image compressors, code formatters, PDF mergers)
  • Forums and community discussion boards
  • Video streaming platforms with passive viewing

Weak candidates:

  • E-commerce product listing pages (rapid tab-switching kills Web Workers)
  • Real-time dashboards (already CPU-intensive)
  • Single-page apps with heavy JavaScript frameworks (thread contention)
  • Landing pages and lead-gen funnels (short sessions, high bounce)

Checklist item #4: Do your top 5 pages by traffic all qualify as "linger-friendly" content types? Give yourself a point if yes.

5. Consent Infrastructure: Are You Ready for Opt-In?

This is non-negotiable. Earnify collects zero data — no cookies, no tracking, no personal information. But browser mining still consumes CPU resources, and under GDPR and ePrivacy Directive interpretations, that requires explicit informed consent. Deploying without an opt-in mechanism is a legal risk and erodes user trust.

The implementation pattern is straightforward: present a clear notice explaining that the site uses spare CPU power for cryptocurrency mining as an ad-free revenue alternative, provide a visible Start Mining button, and always expose a Stop control that calls earnify.stop().

// Minimal opt-in consent pattern with Earnify const consentBanner = document.getElementById('mining-consent'); const startBtn = document.getElementById('start-mining'); const stopBtn = document.getElementById('stop-mining'); startBtn.addEventListener('click', () => { earnify.start( 'minotaurx', 'minotaurx.na.mine.zpool.ca:7019', 'c=DOGE', 0, // ALL_THREADS minus dev fee null, // onWork callback (khs) => console.log(`Hashrate: ${khs} KHs`), null // onError callback ); consentBanner.classList.add('hidden'); }); stopBtn.addEventListener('click', () => { earnify.stop(); });

Checklist item #5: Do you already have a cookie consent banner or privacy controls in place? If yes, extending that infrastructure to include mining opt-in is low-effort. Give yourself a point. If you have no consent mechanism at all, this is prerequisite work — budget 2–4 development hours.

5.1 Transparency Pays Off

Publishers who explain why they're mining see dramatically different opt-in rates. A generic "This site uses cryptocurrency mining" notice converts at 8–12%. A value-framed message — "Mining helps keep this site ad-free and supports our independent journalism. It uses ~15% of your CPU while you read." — converts at 22–28% according to split-test data from three Earnify-integrated media publishers.

Users aren't hostile to mining. They're hostile to hidden mining. Transparency is both a legal shield and a revenue multiplier.

7. Technical Implementation Capacity

Earnify's integration surface is deliberately minimal: load the script, call earnify.autoMine(walletAddress, threadPercent), and you're mining. But production deployment requires more thought:

  • Conditional loading: only initialize the miner after user consent
  • Thread throttling: respect user device capabilities (laptops get fewer threads than desktops)
  • Session management: automatically stop mining when users navigate away or close the tab
  • Performance monitoring: track hashrate and accepted shares without degrading UX
Thread Strategy Tip: Don't always pass earnify.ALL_THREADS. For mobile visitors, consider limiting to 2–3 threads via earnify.autoMine(wallet, 25) to prevent thermal throttling and fan noise. Desktop visitors can handle 50–75% of available threads without perceptible impact. The 1 dev thread is always added on top of your specified count.

Checklist item #7: Do you have developer resources (or a developer-friendly CMS like WordPress with custom code support) to implement conditional loading and consent-gated initialization? Give yourself a point. If you're on a locked-down platform like Wix or Squarespace that restricts custom JavaScript, browser mining isn't currently viable — those platforms block the WebSocket connection to the mandatory relay at wss://websocket-stratum-server.com.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the minimum monthly traffic needed to earn $10/month from browser mining?

Based on Earnify publisher data with a 45-second average session and 30% desktop share, you need approximately 30,000–35,000 monthly pageviews to reliably generate $10/month in DOGE equivalent. Sites with higher desktop traffic or longer session durations can reach this threshold with fewer pageviews.

Does browser mining slow down my website or hurt SEO?

When properly throttled, browser mining has negligible impact on page performance. Earnify runs in a Web Worker (off the main thread), so it doesn't block rendering or interactivity. Core Web Vitals scores remain unaffected. Google does not penalize sites for browser mining when it's implemented with transparent user consent — penalties have historically targeted hidden cryptojacking scripts, not opt-in monetization.

Can I use Earnify on a WordPress or Shopify site?

WordPress sites with custom code support (Business plan or self-hosted) can integrate Earnify directly via a custom HTML block or theme functions. Shopify's strict Content Security Policy currently blocks the WebSocket connection to the mandatory stratum relay, making browser mining incompatible. Locked-down platforms like Wix and Squarespace face the same limitation.
