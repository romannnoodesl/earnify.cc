Pool Fees vs Payout Thresholds: Cheapest Path to DOGE

By Roman Noodles, Founder of Earnify · Updated November 19, 2026

Compare pool fees and payout thresholds for DOGE mining. Learn how to minimize costs with browser mining via Earnify and zpool's MinotaurX pool.

Understanding Pool Fees and Payout Thresholds

Every cryptocurrency miner, whether running a warehouse of ASICs or a single browser tab, faces two unavoidable costs: pool fees and payout thresholds. Pool fees are the percentage the mining pool takes from your earnings as compensation for aggregating hashrate and finding blocks. Payout thresholds are the minimum balance you must accumulate before the pool will send coins to your wallet. For large-scale operations, these numbers are just line items. For small miners—especially those using browser-based mining—they can determine whether you ever see a single DOGE.

The interplay between fees and thresholds is often overlooked. A pool with a low fee but a high payout threshold can trap your earnings for months. Conversely, a slightly higher fee with a lower threshold might get coins into your wallet faster. This article breaks down the real numbers behind zpool’s MinotaurX pool—the default for Earnify—and shows you exactly how to pick the cheapest, fastest path to your first payout.

How Pool Fees Affect Your Bottom Line

Pool fees are straightforward: the pool deducts a percentage from every block reward before distributing shares. For MinotaurX on zpool, the fee is a flat 0.5%. That means for every 1,000 DOGE-equivalent you earn, the pool keeps 5. It’s one of the lowest in the CPU-mining space, and because it’s proportional, it doesn’t discriminate between large and small miners.

Key stat: A 0.5% pool fee on a daily earning of 0.0007 LTC costs you 0.0000035 LTC—negligible. The real barrier isn’t the fee; it’s the threshold.

However, browser miners also contend with the dev fee built into the mining script. Earnify’s model is unique: it reserves exactly one CPU thread for the developer, regardless of how many threads you allocate. This isn’t a percentage split; it’s a fixed overhead. On a 4-core machine, that one dev thread eats 25% of total hashrate. On a 2-core device, it’s a brutal 50%. We’ll quantify that impact shortly.

The Hidden Cost of Payout Thresholds

Payout thresholds are the silent killer of small-miner profitability. If the threshold is too high relative to your hashrate, your earnings sit in the pool’s wallet indefinitely—earning the pool interest, not you. Worse, if you stop mining before reaching the threshold, those funds may be forfeited (zpool does not confiscate, but inactive balances below threshold are effectively inaccessible).

For browser miners, where a single visitor might contribute only a few hours of hashing per day, the threshold must be reachable within a reasonable timeframe—ideally a few weeks. The table below shows why the default DOGE payout is a trap for low-hashrate users.

Earnify’s Dev Fee: The Fixed-Thread Model and Its Impact

Unlike typical mining scripts that skim a percentage of hashrate, Earnify dedicates one entire CPU thread to the developer. The user’s allocated threads run in parallel, but total hashrate is split between user and dev. This means the effective fee percentage varies dramatically with the visitor’s core count.

Earnify 2026 benchmark: A desktop visitor on MinotaurX averages 1,870 H/s total across all threads. The dev thread is not included in the user-facing hashrate metric, so the reported minotaurxHashrate.currentHashrateKHs shows only the user’s share.

On a dual-core laptop, half your hashrate goes to the dev fee, doubling the time to reach any payout. On a modern 8-core desktop, the fee drops to a manageable 12.5%, bringing the LTC threshold within 12 days. This variability means publishers should tailor their payout coin choice to their typical audience hardware.

Optimizing for Small Miners: Choosing the Right Payout Coin

For website publishers integrating Earnify, the default c=DOGE password is a legacy choice that made sense when DOGE’s threshold was lower or hashrates were higher. Today, it’s a dead end for all but the most powerful, always-on mining rigs. Switching to c=LTC slashes the time-to-payout by a factor of 50, making it the only practical option for browser mining.

Why LTC Might Be Your Best Bet

Litecoin’s low threshold (0.01 LTC) and stable value make it ideal for micro-earners. Even a visitor with a modest 4-core CPU can accumulate a payout in two weeks of intermittent mining—say, 2–3 hours per day. That’s a realistic window for a loyal site user. And because LTC transaction fees are negligible, you can then swap it for DOGE or any other coin on an exchange without losing a chunk to network costs.

If you’re targeting an audience with higher-end hardware (8+ cores), BTC becomes viable, offering a larger per-payout value with only a slight increase in wait time. But for the broadest compatibility, LTC is the sweet spot.

Visitor CPU (4 cores) → Earnify (3 user threads, 1 dev) → zpool MinotaurX → LTC payout at 0.01 LTC
Hashrate: 1,402.5 H/s → Daily LTC: 0.0007 → Payout in ~14 days

To change the payout coin in Earnify, simply pass the desired password when calling autoMine():

earnify.autoMine("your_wallet_address", 75);
// Default uses c=DOGE; override by setting the password in the start() call directly
earnify.start("minotaurx", "minotaurx.na.mine.zpool.ca:7019", "c=LTC", 3, onWork, onHashrate, onError);

This one-line change can transform your mining revenue from theoretical to tangible.

FAQs

Why does Earnify default to DOGE if it takes so long to reach payout?

The default c=DOGE was chosen for its broad recognition and meme appeal, and it remains a valid option for users with very high hashrate or those who pool many visitors. However, for most publishers, switching to c=LTC is strongly recommended to achieve payouts in days rather than years.

Can I mine directly to my exchange wallet using Earnify?

Yes. You can use any valid LTC, BTC, or DOGE address, including those from exchanges. Just ensure the address supports the coin you’ve selected via the password field. No additional registration is needed on zpool.

Does Earnify’s dev fee change if I use fewer threads?

The dev fee is always one full CPU thread, regardless of how many user threads you allocate. If you set threadPercent to 50% on a 4-core machine, Earnify will use 2 user threads plus 1 dev thread, making the effective fee 33%. The only way to reduce the fee percentage is to mine on hardware with more cores.
